Extinction Events

🌋

Five times, a lot of life didn't make it.

In the last 500 million years, Earth has experienced five mass extinctions — events so severe that more than 75% of all species on Earth were wiped out each time. Each one caused big changes. Each one also started a new set of possibilities in the world. The animal you are traces through every single one of these bottlenecks. Not all information is 100% agreed upon, but this is what we think we know.

What causes a mass extinction?

The causes vary enormously: glaciation, volcanic eruptions, asteroid impact, even trees. Some struck in years, others unfolded over millions. What they share is change that outpaced evolution — shifts in climate, chemistry, or habitat faster than most species could adapt to. The survivors are rarely the most complex or specialized — they're the most adaptable to sudden change.

Each extinction also created an opportunity. When dominant groups disappear, surviving lineages diversify explosively to fill gaps. The pattern repeats across all five extinction events.

    The bigger picture

    You are here because your ancestors survived all five of these events. Every bottleneck — every time life nearly ended — was passed through by the lineage that led to you.

    Some scientists argue we're living through a sixth mass extinction right now, driven by habitat loss, climate change, and human activity. Current extinction rates are estimated at 100–1,000 times the background rate — the pace at which species normally disappear between mass extinctions.

    Unlike the previous five, this one has a cause that can understand what it's doing.
